# Brand

## What ships

[`limeghost-mark-2026-08-31/`](limeghost-mark-2026-08-31/) holds the mark in
three forms. They are **not interchangeable** — its own README says which goes
where, and using the wrong one at the wrong size is what makes a brand look
careless. `scripts/generate-app-icon.swift` reads two of them and chooses by
size.

## What the other folders are, and why they stay

`limeghost-logo-exploration-…`, `limeghost-bird-frame-comparison-…` and
`limeghost-wolf-lockups-…` are **design iterations, not assets.** Nothing in
them is used by the app. They are the rejected directions — wolves, herons,
falcons, eagles, frames — from the day the mark was made.

They are kept deliberately, for the reason
[ip-and-ownership.md](../ip-and-ownership.md) gives about the rest of this
documentation: *it is itself evidence of authorship and should be preserved,
not trimmed*. A mark's iteration history is what shows it was developed here
rather than found somewhere, which matters if the trademark is ever questioned
or the project is ever sold.

They are about 23 MB. That is the whole reason to be tempted to delete them,
and it is not a good enough one — the repository is small, and the blobs are in
history regardless, so removing them from the tree would save nothing.

**Do not delete these to save space.** If they ever genuinely must go, that is a
history rewrite and a decision for the founder, not a tidy-up.

## The rules that produced the mark

- The accent is `#66DB7D`. The gradient runs roughly `#8FF0A8` to `#1B7A3C` and
  **never approaches black or white** — an earlier version faded to near-black,
  which looked striking on a dark presentation and lost the owl's entire body
  against the app's own near-black chrome.
- One drawing cannot serve every size. Above 32 px the full mark; at 16 and 32
  the face alone, because the ring turns to mud there and takes the face with
  it. That was measured, not assumed.
- The mono version carries its form in **negative space** — the gaps between
  wing sweeps, the eye notches, the ring's inner edge. A flat fill of the full
  mark collapses into a blob, which is how the two-artwork rule was discovered.
